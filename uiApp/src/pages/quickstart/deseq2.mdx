## DESeq2 Interactive Application

This tutorial walks you through using our interactive DESeq2 application for differential gene expression analysis. Our web-based interface eliminates the need for coding or command-line operations—simply upload your data, configure your analysis through intuitive menus, and explore your results through interactive visualizations.

The application is built around [DESeq2](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/DESeq2.html), bringing powerful statistical methods for RNA-seq analysis to your browser with a user-friendly point-and-click workflow.

### What You Can Do

Through this interactive application, you can:

- **Upload and validate** your RNA-seq count matrices with instant feedback on data quality
- **Design your experiment** using simple dropdown menus and checkboxes, supporting both simple and complex multi-factorial designs
- **Explore your data** before analysis with built-in quality control and normalization preview
- **Detect hidden batch effects** using our integrated Surrogate Variable Analysis (SVA) tool
- **Run differential expression analysis** with a single click after configuring your parameters
- **Generate publication-ready visualizations** including PCA plots, heatmaps, volcano plots, and Venn diagrams
- **Download results** in various formats for further analysis or publication

No R programming knowledge required—everything happens through interactive controls and instant visual feedback.

For background on the statistical methods used, see the [DESeq2 documentation](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/vignettes/DESeq2/inst/doc/DESeq2.html) and [SVA workflow](https://www.bioconductor.org/packages/devel/workflows/vignettes/rnaseqGene/inst/doc/rnaseqGene.html#using-sva-with-deseq2)

### Example Dataset

This tutorial uses RNA-seq data from [GEO accession GSE113733](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE113733), which examined transcriptome differences in chemically induced neurons derived from wild type (WT), Actb+/- (HET), and Actb-/- (KO) mouse embryonic fibroblasts. The dataset includes 9 samples (3 biological replicates per condition) and demonstrates how DESeq2 can identify neuronal gene programs affected by Actb knockout during direct reprogramming.

You can use this dataset to follow along with the tutorial, or substitute your own RNA-seq count data.

To begin, you'll upload your RNA-seq count data through our file upload interface. The application accepts CSV files containing raw gene count matrices, which can be generated by GeneCountMerger or exported from other upstream tools.

#### Reviewing Your Data

Once your file uploads successfully, the application displays your count matrix in an interactive table where you can:

- **Browse through your data** using pagination controls
- **Sort columns** by clicking on column headers
- **Search for specific genes** using the built-in search box
- **Identify issues** automatically—the app highlights any duplicate gene names in red and displays them in a separate panel for easy review

Duplicate gene names are automatically handled by appending unique IDs, ensuring your analysis runs smoothly without manual data cleaning.

#### Uploading Custom Metadata

After uploading your count matrix, you'll need to provide sample metadata that describes your experimental conditions. This metadata is essential for setting up your experimental design:

1. **Upload a metadata file** (CSV format) that includes sample names and their corresponding conditions
2. **Map your samples** to their experimental groups, treatments, or time points
3. **Verify the mapping** in the preview table to ensure all samples are correctly annotated

The metadata defines how samples will be grouped for differential expression analysis.

### Setting Up Your Analysis

#### Configuring Your Experimental Design

Before running the analysis, you'll configure your experimental design through an interactive form:

1. **Select your factors** from dropdown menus populated with your sample metadata
2. **Build your design formula** by clicking on condition variables—the app constructs the formula for you
3. **Preview your design** to ensure it matches your experimental setup
4. **Set analysis parameters** including p-value thresholds and fold-change cutoffs
5. **Click "Run DESeq2"** to execute the differential expression analysis

The interface guides you through design formula creation with tooltips and examples, making it accessible even if you're unfamiliar with statistical modeling syntax. Click the help icon for detailed information about design formulas and best practices.

### Quality Control Visualizations

After initializing DESeq2, the application provides comprehensive quality control visualizations to help you assess your data quality and identify potential batch effects or outlier samples.

#### Sample Correlation Heatmap

The QC heatmap shows the correlation between all samples in your dataset:

- **High correlation** (darker colors) between replicates indicates good data quality
- **Low correlation** between different conditions confirms biological differences
- **Unexpected patterns** may reveal batch effects or sample swaps

<ImageSample1 path="/deseq2/image5_QC_heatmap.png"/>

#### Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

The PCA plot provides a visual summary of the major sources of variation in your data:

- **Sample clustering** by experimental groups indicates strong biological signal
- **Outlier detection** helps identify problematic samples
- **Batch effects** become visible as unexpected grouping patterns
- **Interactive exploration** allows you to hover over points to identify samples

<ImageSample1 path="/deseq2/image6_QC_PCA_plot.png"/>



### Setting Up Differential Expression Contrasts

After running DESeq2, you can define specific comparisons (contrasts) between your experimental conditions to identify differentially expressed genes.

#### Selecting Conditions for Comparison

The application provides an intuitive interface for setting up pairwise comparisons:

1. **Choose your reference condition** (control group)
2. **Select your treatment condition** (experimental group)
3. **Set statistical thresholds** for significance (adjusted p-value) and biological relevance (log2 fold change)
4. **Run the contrast analysis** to identify differentially expressed genes

<ImageSample1 path="/deseq2/image7_deseq2_conditions.png"/>

#### Analyzing Multiple Conditions

For experiments with multiple treatment groups, you can perform comprehensive comparisons across all conditions:

- **Set up multiple contrasts** simultaneously
- **Compare results** across different experimental conditions
- **Identify condition-specific genes** that respond uniquely to particular treatments
- **Find common response genes** shared across multiple conditions

<ImageSample1 path="/deseq2/image7_deseq2_multiple_conditions_for_further_analysis.png"/>

### Exploring Your Results

Once the analysis completes, the application generates comprehensive visualizations to help you understand and communicate your findings. Each visualization is interactive and publication-ready.

#### Volcano Plot

The volcano plot displays the relationship between fold change and statistical significance for all genes:

- **Upregulated genes** appear in the upper right (positive fold change, significant p-value)
- **Downregulated genes** appear in the upper left (negative fold change, significant p-value)
- **Hover over points** to see gene names and exact values
- **Click on genes** to highlight them across other visualizations
- **Adjust thresholds** dynamically to refine your gene selection

<ImageSample1 path="/deseq2/image8_volcano_plot_differentially_expressed_genes.png"/>
